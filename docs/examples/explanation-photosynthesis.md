## Final result

Photosynthesis is a process that occurs within plant cells, primarily in the chloroplasts. It involves two main phases: light-dependent reactions and the Calvin cycle [1].

### What Goes into Photosynthesis
What goes into photosynthesis are light energy, carbon dioxide (CO₂), and water (H₂O) [2][3].

### What Comes Out of Photosynthesis
What comes out of photosynthesis are glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) and oxygen (O₂) [2][3].

### Light-Dependent Reactions and Their Location within the Plant Cell
The light-dependent reactions take place in the thylakoids, which are part of the chloroplasts [1][3].

### Calvin Cycle and Its Location within the Plant Cell
The Calvin cycle takes place in the stroma of chloroplasts [1][3].

### 1. Turn 3, from https://www.visiblebody.com/learn/biology/photosynthesis/reactions

Within the chloroplasts of a plant cell, photosynthesis occurs in two main phases: the light-dependent reactions, which take place in the thylakoids, and the light-independent reactions (also known as dark reactions or the Calvin cycle), which take place in the stroma. The interior of the chloroplast contains another membrane—the thylakoid membrane—which is folded to form numerous connected stacks of discs. Each stack is a granum (pl. grana). The light-dependent reactions of photosynthesis take place within the thylakoids, while the light-independent reactions occur in the stroma.
